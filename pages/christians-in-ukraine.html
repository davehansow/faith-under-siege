Who are Ukraine's Christians?

Christianity in Ukraine reaches back more than a thousand years, to the baptism of Kyivan Rus' under Prince Volodymyr in 988. Today Ukraine is one of the most religiously diverse countries in Europe. Most believers are Orthodox, divided mainly between the independent Orthodox Church of Ukraine and the Ukrainian Orthodox Church, historically tied to the Moscow Patriarchate. Millions belong to the Ukrainian Greek Catholic Church and the Roman Catholic Church, and Ukraine is home to one of the largest evangelical Protestant communities in Europe: Baptists, Pentecostals and other evangelical churches.

Ukraine's Christians have lived alongside Jewish and Muslim neighbors, and since 2022 many congregations have become shelters, food banks and evacuation hubs for whole towns.

How Russia's war targets Christians in Ukraine

In the parts of Ukraine that Russian forces occupy, independent religious life has been pushed underground. Investigators, journalists and church leaders have documented a consistent pattern:

  • Churches destroyed and seized. More than 737 religious buildings have been damaged or destroyed, and in occupied areas many church buildings have been taken over or closed.
  • Pastors and priests hunted. At least 54 civilian clergy have been killed. Others have been detained, interrogated or forced into exile, like Pastor Mykhailo Brytsyn.
  • Congregations banned. Evangelical, Greek Catholic and other churches outside the Moscow Patriarchate have been refused registration or outlawed. Ukraine's Greek Catholic leader has said that not a single Catholic priest remains in Russian-occupied territory.
  • Children taken. More than 20,651 Ukrainian children have been confirmed deported or forcibly transferred to Russia, many of them from Christian families.

The U.S. Commission on International Religious Freedom has reported on Russia's religious freedom violations in Ukraine, and Mission Eurasia's Continued War Against Faith report tracks the damage church by church.

Questions

Are Christians persecuted in Ukraine?

In Russian-occupied Ukraine, yes. Churches outside the Moscow Patriarchate have been closed, seized or banned, and pastors and priests have been detained, exiled or killed. In free Ukraine, churches of every tradition worship openly.

How many churches have been destroyed in Ukraine?

More than 737 religious buildings have been damaged or destroyed by Russian aggression, according to the Religion on Fire project as cited by Mission Eurasia (data as of December 2025).
