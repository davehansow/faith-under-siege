Film overview · Logline

In a region once celebrated as the post-Soviet “Bible Belt,” a rapidly growing community of Evangelical and Protestant believers becomes a prime target under Russia’s expanding occupation, enduring seized churches, tortured pastors and abducted children as they struggle to keep their faith alive in secret.

Short synopsis

A Faith Under Siege dives into the harrowing reality facing Ukraine’s Christian communities under Russian occupation. As Russia seeks to absorb Ukraine’s population to restore its former empire status, Evangelical and Protestant believers, viewed as apostates by the Kremlin-aligned Russian Orthodox Church, are singled out for persecution. Churches are destroyed or seized, pastors tortured or killed, and families torn apart through abductions, all in an effort to erase dissenting faiths. Yet even amid these horrors (extrajudicial killings, secret worship services, and tens of thousands of children taken to militarized Russian “summer camps”) a steadfast unity emerges. Christians across denominations stand shoulder to shoulder, aided by international allies who refuse to let their plight go unseen.

Full synopsis

A Faith Under Siege is a haunting and urgent film that chronicles the spiritual and human cost of Russia’s war against Ukraine, focusing on the evangelical community’s resilience and the heartbreaking crisis of abducted children. Before the end of the Cold War, Ukraine was known as the “Bible Belt” of the Soviet Union, and the home of the largest evangelical Baptist community in Europe. As missiles fall and cities are reduced to rubble, Ukraine’s vibrant evangelical churches have emerged as frontline responders, offering aid, shelter and hope amid chaos. Yet they, too, are under threat.

Through gripping personal testimonies and on-the-ground reporting, the film exposes how Russia’s invasion has not only targeted territory but also sought to extinguish Ukraine’s religious and cultural identity. Evangelical pastors have been arrested, worship banned in occupied territories, and church buildings seized or destroyed. In tandem, the Kremlin has orchestrated the mass abduction of over 20,000 Ukrainian children, many from Christian families, removing them to Russia in a campaign critics say amounts to cultural genocide. Featuring survivors, church leaders and international experts, A Faith Under Siege reveals a lesser-told story of the war: how faith communities are resisting both spiritual erasure and the systematic dismantling of family and future. This is a story of courage, conscience, and the struggle to hold on to faith when even the children are being taken.

Cast & crew

The people behind the film

  • Film Director

    Yaroslav Lodygin

    Yaroslav Lodygin is a Ukrainian film director, producer, and screenwriter best known for his award-winning debut feature, The Wild Fields (2018).

  • Producer · Exec. Producer

    Colby Barrett, JD, PE

    Colby Barrett is an entrepreneur, filmmaker, and former U.S. Marine Corps Captain who led infantry and scout/sniper platoons in the Pacific Rim and Middle East.

  • Executive Producer

    Steven E. Moore

    Former chief of staff in the U.S. House of Representatives who has spent most of his time in Ukraine since day five of the war. He leads the Ukraine Freedom Project (UFP), which has delivered 250 tons of food, medical supplies, body armor, and other critical aid to the front.

  • Executive Producer

    Anna Shvetsova

    Anna Shvetsova is the COO of the Ukraine Freedom Project. Raised near the Russian border, she frequently visits the front lines and has engaged with over 100 Congressional offices as an expert on U.S. policy in Ukraine.

  • Producer

    Charlie Sadoff

    Charlie Sadoff is a producer, director, and editor whose most recent film Against All Enemies premiered to critical acclaim at the 2023 Tribeca Film Festival.

  • Impact Producer

    Sarah Makin

    Makin served as Deputy Assistant to President Trump, Senior Director on the National Security Council, and the President’s Senior Advisor on International Religious Freedom. She also served Vice President Mike Pence as Director of Strategic Engagement and External Affairs, and continues to advocate on behalf of persecuted religious minorities around the world.

  • Special Forces Veteran

    Christian Hickey

    Founder and CEO of Triple A Authority Foundation and a 15-year veteran of both the U.S. military, serving as a Marine and Green Beret, and law enforcement. He now applies his tactical and community service experience to global missions, serving on the front lines in Ukraine.

  • Chaplain

    Karl Ahlgren

    Karl Ahlgren is a licensed chaplain in Ukraine and a veteran political advisor with over 40 years of experience in government and advocacy. Since 2023, he has made multiple trips to Ukraine to provide humanitarian support and amplify the voices of persecuted believers.
